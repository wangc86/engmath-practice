What you just saw

The DFT does not measure "the spectrum" of a signal. It measures the signal at $N$ evenly spaced frequencies $k f_s / N$, using exactly $N$ samples and nothing else. Both of those limits are visible on this page.

The frequencies it can see. Press the button that moves the tone onto a bin centre, with the rectangular window selected. One line, nothing either side. Now move the tone by half a bin. The same pure tone spreads into a skirt across the whole spectrum, and the peak itself reads lower than the amplitude you put in. Nothing about the signal changed. What changed is that the $N$ samples no longer contain a whole number of cycles, so the repetition the DFT assumes has a step in it — and a step has components everywhere. This is leakage, and it is the reason a clean measurement can look noisy.

$$ X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} w[n]\, x[n]\, e^{-2\pi i k n / N} $$

What the window does. The window $w[n]$ tapers the ends to zero, so the step disappears. Switch between Rectangular, Hamming, Hann and Blackman with the tone off a bin centre and watch the skirt collapse. It is not free: the gentler the window, the wider the main peak. Narrow peak with far-reaching skirts, or broad peak with clean surroundings — that is the whole trade, and there is no window that wins both.

Zero-padding is not resolution. Choose the two-tone example, set $N$ to 1024, and turn zero-padding up to four times. The curve becomes smooth and detailed, and there is still one peak. Now set zero-padding back to none and raise $N$ to 4096 instead: two peaks. Padding adds samples that carry no new information, so it draws the same continuous spectrum at more points. Resolution comes from the observation time $T = N/f_s$ alone — two frequencies can only be separated if they differ by more than $1/T$.

About the numbers on this page. The spectrum is computed here, in your browser, by the same code that a set of automated tests checks against a plain textbook-definition DFT and against values produced symbolically. The vertical axis is calibrated so that a tone of amplitude $A$ sitting on a bin centre reads exactly $A$ — which is why an off-centre tone visibly reads too low.
